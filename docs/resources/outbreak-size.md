## Synthesizing evidence

### State of the evidence
The body of evidence comprises ten analyses, including peer-reviewed studies, preprints and two online dashboards, posted from 24 May to 10 October 2026. Only McCabe et al., Abbott et al., Epistorm, Oraby et al. and Kim et al. estimate true outbreak size or an upper bound of it. Mooring et al. and Chamla et al. project future trajectories, while the three Verheyden preprints address growth, fatality ratios or forecasting of confirmed counts to give wider context on the question of outbreak size.

### How the estimates compare
Abbott et al. estimate roughly 1.6–3.3× as many infections as laboratory-confirmed cases at 10 October.[^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-1][^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-2][^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-3] McCabe et al. estimated about 280–2,520 cases at 27 May, against 1,031 suspected-or-confirmed reported cases, so the range spans values below and above the reported figure.[^mccabe-2026-2][^mccabe-2026-3] Chamla et al. report that the suspected count peaked at 1,077 on 26 May and was then sharply revised downward, so the laboratory-confirmed series became the reliable comparator;[^chamla-2026-discussion-6] a suspected-or-confirmed denominator such as McCabe et al.'s is therefore not equivalent to a confirmed count. Oraby et al. estimate 1,120 cumulative infections at the fitted rate at which patients leave isolation against 896 confirmed cases as of 17 June, without stating a ratio.[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-results-1][^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-2] The analyses differ in denominator, geography and method, and the denominator alone matters: Chamla et al.'s published quantity is the confirmed-case count, which excludes the unconfirmed and unascertained cases that Abbott et al. and McCabe et al. estimate.[^abbott-2026-comparisons-2]

### Key uncertainties and assumptions

Most of the uncertainty is structural rather than statistical, and four assumptions are shared widely enough that their failure would move several estimates together.

First, the analyses make different assumptions about detection, so their estimates are not measuring quite the same thing. Chamla et al. set the reporting fraction at 1.0, which limits their quantity to confirmed cases;[^chamla-2026-methods-11][^abbott-2026-comparisons-2] Epistorm's second calibration stage allows ascertainment as low as 40%;[^epistorm-2026-methods-7] and Abbott et al. have no line list or reporting-completeness data to anchor detection, so their multiplier of roughly 1.6–3.3 infections per confirmed case is a model-based extrapolation.[^abbott-2026-limitations-11][^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-2]

Second, death-based estimates rest on borrowed parameters. McCabe et al. assume a case fatality ratio and onset-to-death delay consistent with earlier Bundibugyo outbreaks and an assumed epidemic doubling time of 7–14 days (a 10-day doubling time in the main scenario), and the estimated size rises with a lower ratio, faster growth, or a higher share of deaths due to Ebola.[^mccabe-2026-20][^mccabe-2026-4][^mccabe-2026-13] Abbott et al. likewise use weakly informed priors for delays and the ratio, often from other outbreaks, and their posteriors largely track those priors.[^abbott-2026-limitations-3]

Third, travel-based inference assumes every resident is equally likely to travel regardless of infection status, which healthcare-seeking behaviour violates.[^kim-mills-donnelly-2026-main-4] Short trips also let infected travellers return home before detection, and accounting for trip durations raised Kim et al.'s central estimates and widened their confidence intervals.[^kim-mills-donnelly-2026-main-3][^kim-mills-donnelly-2026-main-5]

Finally, model simplifications limit what the estimates can show. Chamla et al. assume homogeneous mixing and uniform response intensity, which oversimplify spatial heterogeneity across health zones, and anchoring on a single confirmed-case datum makes their fit sensitive to that anchor.[^chamla-2026-discussion-1] Oraby et al. add that the undetected burden, postmortem detection and funeral infectiousness cannot be separately identified, so several parameters were fixed or strongly regularized.[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-5]

### What would reduce uncertainty
Chamla et al. and Verheyden and Mudogo both name line-list data as a priority, with Chamla et al. adding contact-tracing data.[^chamla-2026-discussion-11][^verheyden-mudogo-2026a-appendix-8] Additionally, in line with their shared uncertainties, would be outbreak-specific cross-border movement data, because travel-based methods are limited by poorly understood crossing dynamics[^kim-mills-donnelly-2026-main-11] and the export stream in Abbott et al. rests on a traveller volume taken from McCabe et al.[^abbott-2026-methods-19][^abbott-2026-methods-24]

### Open questions
The authors’ open questions converge on better linked data. McCabe et al. call for epidemic size, growth rate, and geography to be characterised with epidemiological and genomic data, while Chamla et al. call for line lists, contact-tracing data, and dynamic mobility estimates.[^mccabe-2026-27][^chamla-2026-discussion-11] Verheyden and Mudogo identify patient-linked records as necessary for causal attribution of fatality differences.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-17]

### What this means for the response
The evidence suggests confirmed counts should be treated as a lower bound, rather than as the true number of cases as the underascertainment rate remains modest-to-substantial.[^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-2][^mccabe-2026-22][^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-results-1]

### Abbott et al. (10 Oct 2026)<br>Estimating the current size of the 2026 DRC Bundibugyo virus outbreak

[Read the analysis](https://epiforecasts.io/BVDOutbreakSize/dev/)

### Overview

This analysis estimates how many people have been infected in the DRC outbreak, reported and unreported, and how that compares with laboratory-confirmed cases. It fits a single statistical model of how infections spread to several streams of situation-report counts and to cases exported to Uganda. The outbreak is estimated to have caused 30% 18406-21567, 60% 16546-23965, 90% 14028-29258 infections to date, reported and unreported.[^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-1] Against the 8978 laboratory-confirmed cases by the cut-off, that is roughly 1.6-3.3× as many infections.[^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-2]

### Estimate

**Headline (joint model, DRC nationally, data as of 10 October 2026):** Cumulative infections, reported and unreported, are estimated at 30% 18406-21567, 60% 16546-23965, 90% 14028-29258.[^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-1] Against the 8978 laboratory-confirmed cases by the cut-off, this is roughly 1.6-3.3× as many infections, so confirmed cases are estimated to capture only a small share of the outbreak.[^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-2]

Other quantities reported at the same cut-off:
- The outbreak is estimated to have begun between 2026-03-01 and 2026-03-27 (90% interval), an elapsed age of 197-223 days.[^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-7]
- The initial reproduction number was 30% 1.91-2.18, 60% 1.79-2.38, 90% 1.59-2.73, and the latest is 30% 1.05-1.2, 60% 0.97-1.3, 90% 0.83-1.52.[^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-8]
- The latest growth rate is 30% 0.003-0.014, 60% -0.002-0.021, 90% -0.013-0.035 per day.[^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-9]
- The case-fatality ratio is 30% 0.46-0.51, 60% 0.43-0.54, 90% 0.39-0.6.[^abbott-2026-summary-dashboard-10]

The page does not give numeric infection estimates for the earlier frozen cut-offs or the per-stream fits in the text provided.

### Methods

**Joint Bayesian multi-patch renewal model:** This is a real-time, joint Bayesian model that began as a replication of McCabe and others (2026)[^abbott-2026-infection-to-confirmed-death-delay-rather-than-the-case-1]. One joint model is fitted to every national data stream and the provincial tables, on a daily grid from a seeding date to the cut-off.[^abbott-2026-methods-5] Each patch runs its own renewal equation, with a reproduction number that is a shared weekly trend times a deviation for that patch.[^abbott-2026-methods-6] Each data stream sees a delayed or thinned view of the same patch infections.[^abbott-2026-methods-7] The streams include suspected and confirmed cases and deaths, laboratory volumes, treatment-centre flows, a digitised symptom-onset curve and Uganda exports. Outbreak size and each system's reporting fraction are estimated jointly.[^abbott-2026-infection-to-confirmed-death-delay-rather-than-the-case-2] It covers the DRC nationally by province, with Uganda exports as an additional data stream. Importation couples the patches by moving a share of each day's new infections from one patch to another.[^abbott-2026-methods-8] The outbreak is assumed to have started from a zoonotic spillover and to have grown through an unobserved cryptic exponential phase.[^abbott-2026-sampler-rejects-rather-than-a-domainerror-1] A genetic bound bounds the cryptic phase from below.[^abbott-2026-at-the-origin-over-t-days-1]

**Health-zone model:** A second model splits the joint model's infections in each patch across the health zones of that patch. It is fitted after the joint model, to the same cut-off, and does not feed back into it.[^abbott-2026-forecast_reported-rounds-it-the-same-way-1] It uses the cumulative confirmed cases and deaths for each health zone from the situation reports. It produces zone-level reproduction numbers and one-week forecasts.

**Single-stream fits, frozen re-fits and forecasts:** Single-stream models are written for each count-based stream, so each stream's posterior outbreak size can be compared with the joint.[^abbott-2026-overflow-1] Frozen re-fits cut the data to an earlier cut-off and re-fit the joint model.[^abbott-2026-infection-to-confirmed-death-delay-rather-than-the-case-3] Forecasts are drawn from the fitted model itself.[^abbott-2026-infection-to-confirmed-death-delay-rather-than-the-case-4]

### Assumptions

**Generation interval and seeding:** The generation interval is a Gamma with a sampled mean and SD, taken from the Ebola virus disease serial interval used as a generation-time proxy (mean 15.3 d, SD 9.3 d).[^abbott-2026-variable-so-the-fitted-knots-and-2] The outbreak is assumed to have started from a zoonotic spillover and grown deterministically through an unobserved cryptic exponential phase.[^abbott-2026-sampler-rejects-rather-than-a-domainerror-1] The origin prior is centred so the implied origin sits in mid-February, with 90% of its mass between mid-January and mid-March.[^abbott-2026-sampler-rejects-rather-than-a-domainerror-3] The initial growth rate has a log-normal prior equivalent to a doubling time centred on 11.7 d.[^abbott-2026-sampler-rejects-rather-than-a-domainerror-4] The authors state the model inherits McCabe et al.'s assumptions of a single zoonotic seed and a generation interval from earlier Ebola outbreaks, which help constrain the estimated outbreak age and early reproduction number.[^abbott-2026-limitations-1]

**Genetic bound on outbreak age:** The TMRCA is treated as a lower bound on the outbreak age, leaving the age free above it.[^abbott-2026-at-the-origin-over-t-days-3][^abbott-2026-at-the-origin-over-t-days-4] The molecular clock is fixed to the rate of the 2013-2016 West African Ebola epidemic.[^abbott-2026-at-the-origin-over-t-days-5] The authors state they fix the clock and do not propagate cross-outbreak or clock uncertainty.[^abbott-2026-at-the-origin-over-t-days-6]

**Response effect on transmission:** The response is assumed to take about three weeks (21 days) to take effect and to be able only to reduce transmission.[^abbott-2026-methods-23] The ramp is fixed in time, with an assumed start and three-week duration, and only its size is estimated.[^abbott-2026-limitations-2]

**Incubation and other delays:** Incubation uses the 2007 Uganda outbreak estimate (mean 6.3 d).[^abbott-2026-quantities-read-it-so-the-gradient-1] Onset-to-admission is a Gamma centred on the Isiro reanalysis posterior (implied mean about 4 d).[^abbott-2026-quantities-read-it-so-the-gradient-4] Onset-to-death is the convolution of onset-to-admission and admission-to-death (implied mean about 13 d).[^abbott-2026-quantities-read-it-so-the-gradient-5] The authors state that nearly all of the delays rest on weakly-informed priors, often from other outbreaks, and that their posteriors largely track those priors.[^abbott-2026-limitations-3] The onset-to-report delay is the exception, informed by the onset curve.[^abbott-2026-limitations-4]

**Case-fatality ratio:** The prior is Beta(6.6, 13.4)[^abbott-2026-quantities-read-it-so-the-gradient-6], with a mean that matches the CDC figure and the corrected central CFR in the 20 May report (McCabe and others, 2026).[^abbott-2026-quantities-read-it-so-the-gradient-7] The authors say the CFR prior's posterior largely tracks the prior.[^abbott-2026-limitations-3]

**Ascertainment and non-BVD background:** Two surveillance systems, DRC passive community surveillance and Uganda point-of-entry/hospital surveillance, each capture a fraction of the true cases passing through them.[^abbott-2026-eps-floors-the-sd-so-a-1] The prior reflects the active case-finding of a declared Ebola response.[^abbott-2026-eps-floors-the-sd-so-a-2] The code centres the prior at logit(0.75)[^abbott-2026-eps-floors-the-sd-so-a-3]. Reported suspected cases include an additive non-BVD background.[^abbott-2026-eps-floors-the-sd-so-a-4] The background is inferred to be the majority of the suspect pool.[^abbott-2026-eps-floors-the-sd-so-a-5] The DRC ascertainment, testing fraction and specimens analysed per suspect are each one value over the window, although ascertainment probably rose, so a change in any of them is read as a change in incidence.[^abbott-2026-limitations-5]

**Laboratory assumptions:** Confirmation runs on the altona RealStar Filovirus Screen RT-PCR.[^abbott-2026-eps-floors-the-sd-so-a-6] The effective process sensitivity is about 98% with two controls, so the sensitivity prior is centred there.[^abbott-2026-eps-floors-the-sd-so-a-7] The severity-enrichment priors are not set from data.[^abbott-2026-limitations-6]

**Cross-border travel:** The source population is fixed, and the traveller volume is given a Normal prior around the McCabe et al. figure.[^abbott-2026-methods-24] The export model counts every border crossing by an undetected infection as a possible detection in Uganda. A constant share of round trips is absorbed into the Uganda ascertainment, but a share that changes over the window is not.[^abbott-2026-limitations-7]

**Spatial structure and stream independence:** The streams observe overlapping people but are fitted as conditionally independent given latent incidence, which can understate uncertainty.[^abbott-2026-limitations-8] Ituri, Nord-Kivu and Haut-Uele are modelled individually, every other affected province is pooled into a fourth patch, and each is well mixed.[^abbott-2026-limitations-9] The gravity kernel coupling the provinces is assumed, not measured, as there is no mobility or origin-destination data.[^abbott-2026-limitations-10]

### Limitations

**Aggregate data and model-based extrapolation:** The authors do not have a line list, information on case definitions or reporting completeness, or access to the response's internal data. Every estimate is therefore a model-based extrapolation under strong assumptions, not a measurement.[^abbott-2026-limitations-11] The work is done outside the outbreak response, so changes in reporting practice are inferred from the reports rather than known.[^abbott-2026-limitations-12] Almost every count is report-dated, so the epidemic's timing is recovered mainly through the assumed delays.[^abbott-2026-limitations-13]

**Status of the analysis:** The code and analysis were drafted by a language model and reviewed by people and by language models.[^abbott-2026-limitations-14] The report is re-run as new data arrive, so the estimates change between updates, and it has not been peer reviewed.[^abbott-2026-limitations-15]

### Verheyden and Mudogo (13 Sep 2026)<br>Observed Fatality or Observed Death? Differential Death Ascertainment and Provincial Mortality Heterogeneity during the 2026 Bundibugyo Virus Disease Epidemic in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

[Read the analysis](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7574638)

### Overview

This analysis does not attempt to estimate the true size of the 2026 BVD outbreak or a detection multiplier for total infections. Instead, it investigates why North Kivu reported a substantially higher cumulative case-fatality ratio (CFR) than Ituri Province, using a longitudinal reconstruction of DRC national situation reports (SitReps) through 13 September 2026. The central finding is that 415/1,507 (27.5%) classified positives in Ituri and 265/568 (46.7%) in North Kivu were already deceased when reported positive (Mantel-Haenszel OR 2.18, 95% CI 1.76-2.70)[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-abstract-1], indicating that differential ascertainment (detection of cases alive versus after death) is a major driver of the provincial CFR gap rather than necessarily reflecting a true outbreak-size discrepancy.

### Estimate

The analysis does not produce an estimate of true outbreak size or a detection multiplier relative to confirmed cases. It reports only reported/confirmed case and death counts and CFR-related quantities. For context, confirmed cumulative counts at the freeze were by 13 September, the formal freeze, Ituri reported 5,659 cases and 2,583 deaths (45.6%) and North Kivu 1,281 cases and 797 deaths (62.2%), a 16.6-point gap[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-results-3]. The key quantitative outputs are the dead-at-detection proportions (27.5% Ituri vs 46.7% North Kivu, Mantel-Haenszel OR 2.18, 95% CI 1.76–2.70) and the counterfactual compatibility calculation showing that applying a common 25% fatality rate among live-detected cases to the late-regime dead-at-detection shares would generate expected reported CFRs of 45.7% and 61.5%, respectively[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-results-4], close to the observed 13 September CFRs. No estimate of true infection numbers or under-ascertainment multiplier for total case burden is given.

### Methods

**Longitudinal ecological surveillance reconstruction:** The authors designed a longitudinal ecological surveillance analysis using official DRC Institut National de Santé Publique (INSP) situation reports from the 17th Ebola epidemic[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-4], organized at province-by-SitRep level, to compare the proportion of newly confirmed cases already dead when detected ("dead-at-detection share") between Ituri and North Kivu provinces.

**Matched-SitRep comparison with Mantel-Haenszel and Bayesian binomial logistic modeling:** The core analysis restricted comparisons to SitReps in which both Ituri and North Kivu contain definitionally compatible living/deceased splits[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-5], then used a Mantel-Haenszel comparison stratified by SitRep[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-6] alongside a Bayesian binomial logistic model...fitted as a complementary analysis with province, reporting regime, and their interaction[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-7], applied to the same two-province dataset of 28 matched SitReps.

**Counterfactual standardization (compatibility calculation):** This method used a simple accounting identity, If π is the proportion of confirmed cases already deceased when detected and q is the eventual fatality among cases detected alive, then under a two-pathway completed-outcome accounting model the expected reported CFR is R = π + q(1-π)[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-8], applied to the same Ituri/North Kivu provincial data to test whether ascertainment differences alone could reproduce the observed CFR gap, varying q from 0.10 to 0.50.

**Postmortem-surveillance and death-location triangulation:** Using operational indicators such as bodies swabbed relative to death alerts and death-location classification (community, health facility, CTE/CT), the analysis triangulated independent evidence of differential postmortem detection between the two provinces, e.g. in the three matched SitReps for which a comparable death-alert denominator and number of bodies swabbed could be reconstructed (72, 80 and 82), Ituri reported 163 bodies swabbed for 247 death alerts (66.0%), whereas North Kivu reported 118 for 120 (98.3%)[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-results-2].

None of these methods estimate a true outbreak size or a detection multiplier for total infections; the analysis is explicit that it does not attempt latent incidence estimation: We did not fit a more elaborate latent epidemic simulation because the aggregate SitRep streams do not independently identify true incidence, outcome-specific detection probabilities, and biological fatality.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-9]

### Assumptions

**Two-pathway accounting structure:** The counterfactual calculation assumes cases enter the confirmed series via only two pathways — detected alive or detected after death — formalized as If π is the proportion of confirmed cases already deceased when detected and q is the eventual fatality among cases detected alive, then under a two-pathway completed-outcome accounting model the expected reported CFR is R = π + q(1-π)[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-8]. The authors are explicit this is a compatibility analysis, not an estimator of infection-fatality risk[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-10].

**Common eventual fatality (q) across provinces:** The compatibility calculation assumes a shared eventual fatality probability among live-detected cases, tested across q values from 0.10 to 0.50[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-11], with an illustrative common value of 25% used to reproduce observed CFRs. **Non-identifiability of latent incidence and fatality:** The analysis structurally assumes it cannot and should not attempt to decompose true incidence, biological fatality, and ascertainment from aggregate counts alone, stating with aggregate SitRep counts alone, true incidence, biological fatality, live-case detection, and postmortem detection are not separately identifiable without strong external constraints[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-12], and treating this as a deliberate stop rule: when materially different combinations of latent incidence, fatality, and ascertainment can generate the same observed streams, the data do not support a unique decomposition[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-13].

**Stratification by reporting regime rather than a constant province effect:** Because the report-specific odds were heterogeneous, we did not interpret one common odds ratio as a time-invariant biological effect[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-14], the analysis assumes the ascertainment contrast varies by reporting regime (COUSP period SitReps 66-77 vs Task Force period 96-122) rather than being constant over time, and models this with a Bayesian province-by-regime interaction using weakly informative priors Weakly informative Normal(0, 2.5) priors were used for contrast coefficients and a wider Normal(0, 5) prior for the intercept[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-15].

**Missingness treated as non-random and non-imputed:** The analysis assumes missing fields should not be filled in, stating Missingness was treated as a feature of the surveillance record. We did not impute zero for absent fields and did not interpolate the primary living/deceased split across reports.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-16]

### Limitations

**Aggregate, unlinked data:** The authors state that the SitReps are aggregate operational reports rather than linked patient-level datasets, so a death classified in the laboratory section cannot be linked to an alert, admission, treatment history, symptom-onset date, or individual outcome trajectory.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-discussion-2]

**Changing definitions and reconciliation:** The authors note reporting definitions changed over time, some reports contain reconciliation corrections, and several operational fields are absent or incomplete.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-discussion-3] Relatedly, the primary split is available only on selected reports and may itself reflect which samples reached the laboratory.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-discussion-4]

**Non-identifiability of biological fatality:** The authors emphasize that neither the matched analysis nor the counterfactual standardization identifies the true infection-fatality risk. A latent decomposition would require patient-linked data or strong external constraints; without them, multiple combinations of incidence, mortality, and ascertainment remain observationally compatible.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-discussion-5]

**Operational indicators not patient-linked:** Postmortem swabbing and death-alert counts used for triangulation are described as limited: these are aggregate operational completion indicators; alert and swab counts are not patient-linked and can include carry-over.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-results-7]

**Cannot rule out genuine clinical/capacity differences:** The authors acknowledge the source-audited and model-based analyses support an ascertainment mechanism but also leave a credible pathway through which delayed presentation and constrained care could contribute to genuine mortality differences.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-results-8]

**Health-zone analysis limited:** Health-zone data confirm that neither province is internally homogeneous, but the current SitRep series does not provide the primary living/deceased laboratory split consistently at health-zone level.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-results-1]

**Cannot quantify effect of parallel administrative structure:** Regarding the AFC/M23-administered response in parts of North Kivu, the authors state we cannot quantify this pathway from the available aggregate SitReps, and offer it as one plausible, non-mutually-exclusive mechanism rather than an identified cause.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-discussion-6]

### Data sources

Primary outbreak data: the analysis relies on official DRC Institut National de Santé Publique (INSP) situation reports from the 17th Ebola epidemic[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-methods-4], with twenty-eight SitReps provid[ing] a matched provincial living/deceased laboratory split[^verheyden-mudogo-2026b-results-5] forming the primary inferential series (SitReps 66, 67, 69, 70, 72, 73, 74, 77, 96, 97, 99, 101, 102, 104, 105, 108, 109, 110, 111, 112, 113, 114, 115, 116, 117, 118, 119 and 122, per the document). No travel, demographic, or cross-border datasets are used, as the analysis is confined to within-DRC provincial surveillance comparison.

**GLEAM metapopulation model with care-pathway SEIHFR dynamics:** This is a stochastic, spatially explicit simulation model in which GLEAM (Global Epidemic and Mobility Model) is an Africa-wide metapopulation network coupled by daily commuting matrices and origin-destination passenger flows, with within-basin compartmental dynamics simulated as a discrete-time multinomial chain-binomial process[^epistorm-2026-methods-4]. **Two-stage Approximate Bayesian Computation calibration:** The model's epidemiological parameters are fitted to outbreak data using ABC rather than a direct estimator. This analysis aims to characterize, rather than estimate a single "true size" figure for, the 2026 BDBV outbreak: specifically its early growth rate, the date transmission actually began (back-calculated), and whether the rising case-fatality ratio reflects worsening severity or a detection artefact. This analysis does not produce a single "true outbreak size" figure comparable to under-ascertainment models; its primary quantitative outputs are growth rate, doubling time, and back-calculated onset date. **Classical growth-rate regression:** Cumulative case counts C(t) were modelled as C(t) = C₀·exp(r·t), where r is the exponential growth rate and doubling time Tᵈ = ln(2)/r; the early-phase model (14-27 May, pre-discontinuity) was fitted by non-linear least squares to 12 daily observations, and a logistic model was fitted to the full 65-observation series and compared against the exponential model by Akaike Information Criterion.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026a-methods-1] This method is applied to DRC-national confirmed daily case counts.

**Bayesian hierarchical growth/back-calculation model:** A Bayesian hierarchical model (Poisson likelihood, weakly informative Normal priors, fitted by Markov Chain Monte Carlo in PyMC3) estimated growth rate r, initial case count C₀, and t_back - the number of days before the first situation report that transmission began - with full posterior uncertainty propagation.[^verheyden-mudogo-2026a-methods-2] (The verified implementation used a Normal rather than Poisson likelihood with an estimated observation-noise scale.) ### Assumptions

### Overview

This analysis is a retrospective forecasting evaluation, not an under-detection or "true size" estimation study: it tests how accurately simple and more complex statistical models could have forecast the already-confirmed case and death trajectory of the 2026 BVD outbreak using only data available in real time. The authors refitted two prespecified operational models using only data available at that time: a recent seven-increment baseline and a Bayesian negative-binomial surveillance-maturity model fitted by full Markov Chain Monte Carlo on complete available history[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-abstract-1], alongside a Gompertz growth model...fitted identically at every origin as a phenomenological benchmark[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-abstract-2]. The main finding is that Routine SitRep data supported useful short-term forecasting, but no single model was best on every criterion, and a data-driven combination of two complementary, individually weaker-in-some-respect models measurably outperformed either alone for one of two outcomes evaluated[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-abstract-3]; this paper does not independently estimate a "true" outbreak size distinct from confirmed counts.

### Estimate

This analysis does not produce an independent "true outbreak size" estimate distinct from confirmed/reported counts; it forecasts future values of the confirmed case and death series itself. The headline forward-looking output is from the most recent origin: Forecasts from the most recent eligible origin (20 July 2026; 2,473 cumulative confirmed cases, 999 cumulative confirmed deaths) are reported for all three horizons as a genuinely forward-looking scenario, not yet scoreable against observation[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-results-1]. Within that output, the authors flag one figure as an upper-scenario extrapolation: The 21-day Bayesian death forecast (median 2,286.8, more than double the 999 deaths recorded at the origin) is reported without adjustment because it is a faithful output of the validated, checkpoint-verified pipeline, but is flagged here as an aggressive extrapolation that should be read as an upper scenario rather than a precise point estimate[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-results-2], noting that the ensemble (not tabulated here; see Table 2 for validated weights) provides a more conservative central figure by construction for this reason[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-results-3]. ### Methods

**Baseline recent-trend model:** This is a simple operational forecasting rule applied to the national confirmed-case and death series. The baseline model assumed that the recent mean reporting pace would continue over the forecast horizon. For valid one-calendar-day increments...the expected daily increment at origin T was the mean of the seven most recent valid increments.[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-materials-and-methods-2] Predictive intervals came from bootstrap resampling of the recent valid increments (20,000 replicates), with resampled negative increments truncated at zero[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-materials-and-methods-3], producing a point forecast and interval for confirmed cases/deaths at national level.

**Bayesian negative-binomial surveillance-maturity model:** This is a probabilistic count model fitted to the complete national daily incidence history at each forecast origin, explicitly representing a detected change in surveillance capacity. For valid daily incident counts over the complete available history at each forecast origin...S is an indicator equal to 1 on and after 28 May 2026 and 0 before it; the level shift attributable to the surveillance-maturity discontinuity; separate trend slopes for the post- and pre-discontinuity regimes; serial dependence on the previous observed and expected count (an INGARCH-type structure); and the negative-binomial dispersion parameter[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-materials-and-methods-4]. **Gompertz growth curve (phenomenological benchmark):** Cumulative cases or deaths were modelled using the Gompertz function C(t) = K·exp{−a·exp(−bt)}, refitted at each origin by non-linear least squares subject to positivity constraints, with predictive uncertainty from parametric bootstrap.[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-materials-and-methods-5] This model was tested at national level but is not carried forward as a recommended forecasting tool: its 80% predictive-interval coverage was the poorest of all candidates at every horizon and outcome evaluated[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-materials-and-methods-6].

**Data-driven ensemble:** This method pools simulated trajectories from the baseline and recalibrated Bayesian model rather than averaging published intervals. **Recalibration and ensemble validation:** The recalibration correction and ensemble weights are selected using a prospective, no-lookahead procedure (median forecast error over earlier mature origins) but are both derived and tested on the same 55 origins, rather than on a fully independent dataset, as discussed under Limitations.

**Data gaps and discrepancies:** Seven calendar dates lack a national confirmed-case observation...and are retained as explicitly missing rather than imputed or interpolated[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-materials-and-methods-11], and a one-case discrepancy was identified and corrected during cross-checking, which the authors note is smaller than the between-run resampling noise already documented for this pipeline (±10 units) and did not warrant refitting[^verheyden-mudogo-jacquet-2026-materials-and-methods-12].

The model produces posterior estimates of R0, the leakage rate, detection probability, and projected cumulative "actual infections" (reported plus unreported) via counterfactual simulation, applied at the Ituri provincial level as a single homogeneously-mixing population.

**Forward counterfactual scenario simulation**: Using the fitted posterior, the authors ran scenario analyses varying leakage rate, re-isolation rate, isolation speed, detection intensity, and safe-burial ascertainment to project cumulative actual infections, reported cases, and deaths under each scenario, again scoped to Ituri Province. Control measures were evaluated using forward counterfactual simulation based on the fitted posterior and without re-estimation.[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-6]

### Assumptions

**Fixed natural-history and funeral-process parameters**: The authors fix the natural-history and funeral-process parameters, T_U, c_U, γ_U, µ_U, ν, ν_H, and m_F, using externally specified community infectious duration T_U and community fatality probability c_U to reduce structural non-identifiability in the undetected and funeral-transmission pathways[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-13], justified because the reported case and death time series cannot separately identify the undetected burden, the community fatality probability, the death-detection probability, and the funeral infectiousness[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-14].

**R0 prior informed by CDC estimate**: The model places a prior for the basic reproduction number... which places most prior mass near the CDC's current outbreak interquartile range[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-15], while explicitly noting the CDC branching-process model differs structurally from our compartmental detection-isolation-leakage model[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-16].

**Equal transmissibility across pathways**: The baseline model assumes β_I = β_U, β_L = β_U, and β_F = m_F·β_U with m_F ≥ 1[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-17], meaning detected pre-isolation, undetected, and leaked individuals are assumed equally infectious until isolation.

**No hospital-acquired transmission and homogeneous mixing**: Hospital-acquired transmission is intentionally assumed to be zero; patients in the hospital are considered completely and effectively isolated.[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-1] The model also does not incorporate spatial structure, age-stratified contact patterns, or explicit hospital-acquired transmission, instead representing the Ituri epicenter as a single, homogeneously mixing population[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-2].

**Biological consistency constraint on leakage sojourn**: The model imposes the biological consistency constraint that the total expected time from hospitalization to disease resolution is the same regardless of whether the patient remains in H or escapes to L[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-18].

**Fixed population size**: The affected population is fixed at N(0) ≈ 2,000,000, approximating the Ituri health zones with active transmission[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-5].

### Limitations

**Under-recording of leakage**: The leakage pathway relies on reported escapee counts and the isolation census, both of which are likely under-recorded, so the true magnitude of leakage may be underestimated[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-3], particularly since escapes that occur at night and incidents that occur during security disruptions are unlikely to be recorded consistently[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-4].

**Structural non-identifiability**: Structural non-identifiability involving the undetected burden, the community fatality probability, the postmortem detection probability, and funeral infectiousness forced the authors to fix or strongly regularize several natural-history and funeral-process parameters; sensitivity analyses constrain, but do not eliminate, this dependence[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-5], and weak identifiability of m_F and ϕ_D implies that conclusions regarding the funeral pathway should be regarded as tentative[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-6].

**Equal transmissibility assumption**: The model assumes undetected infections, pre-isolation infections, and infections arising from escapees have the same transmissibility[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-7], though the authors note individuals who escape typically do so at later stages of illness when infectiousness is highest, so this would increase, rather than decrease, the burden attributable to leakage, meaning their estimates are conservative[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-8].

**Overstated contact follow-up and detection ceiling**: Reported proportions of contact follow-up may exaggerate the number of contacts truly reached in contexts of insecurity, displacement, and mistrust, so the high estimated detection probabilities (p_d ≈ 0.91-0.99) should be interpreted as upper limits on actual case ascertainment[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-9].

**No spatial structure or heterogeneity**: The model does not incorporate spatial structure, age-stratified contact patterns, or explicit hospital-acquired transmission, representing Ituri as a single homogeneously mixing population, despite substantial heterogeneity in access to care and reporting likely in a context of armed conflict and mass displacement[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-10][^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-11].

**Idealized intervention levers**: The intervention analysis treats each control parameter as an idealized lever adjustable independently; efforts such as a two- to three-fold increase in isolation speed may be limited by insecurity, ambulance capacity, and community resistance, and levers are evaluated over fixed ranges rather than cost or feasibility, so the ranking captures epidemiological potential rather than public health practicality.[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-12][^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-13]

**No vaccination or therapeutics**: Vaccination and therapeutics are not included in the model, consistent with the current lack of a licensed BDBV vaccine or approved treatment.[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-discussion-14]

### Data sources

**Primary outbreak data**: The core input is the official Situation Reports issued by the Centre d'Opérations d'Urgence de Santé Publique (COUSP) for the period from 14 May to 17 June 2026 (31 situation reports, N° 001-N° 034)[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-1], covering confirmed cases, deaths, discharges, isolation census, and contact follow-up. Over this window, cumulative confirmed cases rose from 8 to 896 and confirmed deaths from 4 to 232, with new confirmed cases per inter-report window averaging 30.6 (maximum 107)[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-8]. Escapee-specific data come from the Bunia health-zone cluster, where escapees (évadés) were recorded on every day of observation from 20 May to 1 June 2026, with a total of 60 leakage events across 13 days at 13 different facilities, and a highest single-day count of 10[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-introduction-4]; provincially, escapees were reported on 24 of the reporting days, totaling 175, with a single-day maximum of 23[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-9], and the escapee stream contributes to 15 reports (1-17 June 2026)... with 97 escapees in total[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-10]. The national isolation census, available from 1 June onward, ranged from 173 to 383 patients[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-11].

**Auxiliary data**: Natural-history parameters are drawn from literature values from the 2007 BDBV Uganda outbreak and from a systematic review of Ebola modeling parameters[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-12], including a BDBV-specific subgroup random-effects mean incubation period estimated at 7.65 days (95% CI 6.43-8.88)[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-s1-supplementary-material-1] from a sample of n = 116[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-s1-supplementary-material-2] studies, and hospitalization duration T_H = 10.8 days for in-isolation recovery... based on the BDBV-specific estimate[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-s1-supplementary-material-3]. The R0 prior is informed by a CDC scenario-projection analysis of the current 2026 Bundibugyo virus disease outbreak reported a calibrated median R0 = 2.51 with an interquartile range of 2.27-2.82, based on 50 deaths by 24 May 2026[^oraby-falay-ndeffo-mbah-2026-methods-and-materials-3].

### Chamla et al. (8 Jun 2026)<br>Size of the 2026 Ebola outbreak and risk of cross-border spillover from Bundibugyo virus in Ituri Province, DR Congo, and its implications for preparedness: a recalibrated stochastic modelling study

[Read the analysis](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laninf/article/PIIS1473-3099(26)00320-8/fulltext)

### Overview

This analysis estimates the short-term epidemic trajectory of the 2026 BDBV outbreak in Ituri Province, DRC, and the probability of cross-border spillover into neighbouring countries. It uses a stochastic SEIRD (susceptible, exposed, infectious, recovered, dead) ensemble model calibrated to 598 cumulative laboratory-confirmed cases as of 8 June 2026, with simulation filtering to retain model runs consistent with that anchor.[^chamla-2026-summary-1] Under the central scenario (R0 = 1.71), the model projects a median of 990 cumulative confirmed cases by week 12 (24 June 2026; 90% prediction interval 709 to 1,293) and 174 deaths, with cross-border importation into Uganda assessed as nearly certain (94.2%) and into South Sudan as highly probable (69.3%).[^chamla-2026-summary-2]

### Estimate

Under the central transmissibility scenario (mean accepted R0 = 1.71), the ensemble projected a median of 648 cumulative confirmed cases by week 10 (8 June 2026; 50% PI 560 to 732), rising to 990 by week 12 (24 June 2026; 50% PI 858 to 1,141 and 90% PI 709 to 1,293), with a projected median of 174 deaths at week 12.[^chamla-2026-results-2]

Under the low-transmissibility scenario (mean accepted R0 = 1.42), cumulative confirmed cases reached a median of 870 by week 12 (50% PI 752 to 1,004 and 90% PI 641 to 1,133; 160 deaths) and 3,662 by week 24 (50% PI 2,884 to 4,484 and 90% PI 2,094 to 6,037; 673 deaths).[^chamla-2026-results-3]

Under the high-transmissibility scenario (mean accepted R0 = 2.08), confirmed cases reached a median of 1,364 (50% PI 1,154 to 1,579 and 90% PI 975 to 1,807; 202 deaths) by week 12, rising to 66,420 (50% PI 56,560 to 78,937 and 90% PI 41,718 to 95,881; 10,644 deaths) by week 24.[^chamla-2026-results-4]

Under the central scenario, week 24 projects a median of 8,210 cumulative confirmed cases (50% PI 6,636 to 10,287 and 90% PI 5,063 to 13,498) and approximately 1,440 deaths if transmission were merely sustained.[^chamla-2026-results-5]

The observed confirmed-case trajectory is consistent with the central scenario; the high scenario is retained as an upper-bound contingency should control measures lapse.[^chamla-2026-results-6] Importantly, this analysis calibrates exclusively to laboratory-confirmed cases and does not produce a separate "true outbreak size" estimate that exceeds the confirmed count; the reporting fraction was set at 1.0 because laboratory-confirmed cases are, by definition, ascertained, and the earlier suspected-case reporting fraction assumption is no longer required.[^chamla-2026-methods-11] No detection multiplier is calculated or reported.

### Methods

**Stochastic SEIRD ensemble model:** A discrete-time SEIRD framework with daily transitions implemented as binomial draws using standard mass-action incidence, applied to the DRC outbreak (Ituri and Nord Kivu provinces combined). Three transmissibility scenarios span published BDBV estimates: low R0 = 1.42, central R0 = 1.71, and high R0 = 2.08.[^chamla-2026-methods-4][^chamla-2026-methods-5][^chamla-2026-methods-6] Calibration was performed by simulation filtering (approximate rejection sampling, not formal Bayesian inference): replicates were accepted if cumulative laboratory-confirmed cases at day 68 (8 June 2026) fell within ±30% of 598 (range 419 to 777), retaining 400 calibration-passing replicates per scenario.[^chamla-2026-methods-7] Sensitivity analyses examined travel-while-infectious (5% to 25%), reporting completeness (0.60 to 0.90), and catchment size (250,000 to 500,000).[^chamla-2026-methods-8]

**Daily-hazard spillover model:** Importation risk to Uganda, South Sudan, Rwanda, and Burundi was modelled as a daily Poisson hazard driven by calibrated infectious prevalence from the SEIRD model, country-specific cross-border mobility, point-of-entry detection sensitivity from WHO Joint External Evaluation assessments, and travel-while-infectious parameters (Uganda 12%; South Sudan 15%; Rwanda and Burundi 8%), with full equations and parameter values given in an appendix.[^chamla-2026-methods-9] Importation probability was computed as 1 − exp(cumulative 12-week hazard), yielding the probability of at least one importation for each country.[^chamla-2026-methods-10]

### Assumptions

**Transmissibility scenarios:** Three R0 values (low 1.42, central 1.71, high 2.08) span published BDBV estimates. Response-intensity reductions applied from 15 May 2026 are 20% (low scenario), 25% (central), and 15% (high), reflecting differing assumptions about operational effectiveness.[^chamla-2026-methods-6][^chamla-2026-methods-14]

**Latent and infectious periods:** The latent period is fixed at 9.7 days and the infectious period at 5.0 days, in line with the broader filovirus literature. These are not varied across scenarios.[^chamla-2026-methods-15]

**Case-fatality ratio:** Drawn from a uniform distribution (0.15 to 0.25), centred on the observed confirmed-case ratio of approximately 19% (115 of 598 as of 8 June 2026) and consistent with the historical BDBV range.[^chamla-2026-methods-16]

**Ascertainment and reporting fraction:** Set at 1.0 for laboratory-confirmed cases on the structural assumption that confirmed cases are fully ascertained by definition. This eliminates the need to estimate an under-detection multiplier but means the model tracks the confirmed trajectory only, not total infections.[^chamla-2026-methods-11]

**Homogeneous mixing and catchment size:** The model assumes homogeneous mixing within a single catchment of 350,000 across seven affected health zones, including Ituri and Nord Kivu. Spatial heterogeneity is not captured, acknowledged by the authors as a stated limitation.[^chamla-2026-methods-17]

**Calibration tolerance:** Simulation filtering accepts replicates if cumulative confirmed cases at day 68 fall within ±30% of 598 (range 419 to 777). The ±30% tolerance was chosen pragmatically given residual data uncertainty, and is not derived from formal inference.[^chamla-2026-methods-18][^chamla-2026-methods-19]

**Cross-border mobility and travel-while-infectious parameters:** The spillover model uses country-specific travel-while-infectious parameters (Uganda 12%; South Sudan 15%; Rwanda and Burundi 8%) alongside WHO Joint External Evaluation-based detection sensitivity. The authors acknowledge these parameters were not empirically derived for this outbreak and represent the principal sources of uncertainty in spillover projections.[^chamla-2026-methods-3][^chamla-2026-methods-20]

**No vaccination compartment:** No vaccinated compartment was included, reflecting the absence of a licensed BDBV-specific vaccine.[^chamla-2026-methods-21]

**Model type:** These projections are described by the authors as exploratory preparedness-oriented estimates derived from a stochastic scenario-based modelling framework, rather than predictions generated from formally fitted epidemiological models using comprehensive parameter estimation and identifiability analyses.[^chamla-2026-summary-5]

### Limitations

**Homogeneous mixing and absent spatial structure:** The model oversimplifies spatial heterogeneity across health zones, does not separately model healthcare-worker transmission, and applies response intensity uniformly rather than varying it by place, time, access, or community acceptance.[^chamla-2026-discussion-1]

**Single-point calibration anchor:** The model is anchored on a single recent confirmed-case datum (598 on 8 June 2026), making the fit sensitive to that anchor.[^chamla-2026-discussion-1]

**Simulation filtering is not formal inference:** Calibration by approximate rejection sampling is acknowledged as not constituting formal Bayesian inference, and informal cross-border mobility estimates are described as imprecise.[^chamla-2026-discussion-1]

**Uniform and static response intensity:** The response-intensity factor was applied uniformly from 15 May, but real-world response effectiveness varies by place, time, access, and community acceptance. Live mobility data and behavioural adaptation were not incorporated.[^chamla-2026-discussion-2]

**Surveillance dynamics and residual confounding:** The post-declaration case-finding surge probably contributed to rapid apparent growth between 15 and 19 May; the analysis addressed this by lowering the reporting fraction and widening the calibration window, but residual confounding between transmission intensity and surveillance intensity remains.[^chamla-2026-discussion-3]

**Early-phase data quality:** Calibration initially relied on early-phase surveillance counts from the first weekly WHO External Situation Report, where the suspected-case total was only partially resolved into laboratory-confirmed cases and death counts reflected surveillance signals rather than confirmed mortality alone. Misclassification, delayed laboratory throughput, and ongoing active case finding could each shift the calibration anchor in either direction. The projections are therefore interpreted as an early situational assessment rather than a definitive forecast.[^chamla-2026-discussion-4]

**Need for iterative recalibration:** The projections require iterative recalibration as new data emerge.[^chamla-2026-discussion-5]

### Data sources

**Primary outbreak data:** Case data were taken from WHO AFRO External Situation Reports and the WHO AFRO internal tracker through 8 June 2026. The laboratory-confirmed case series used as the primary calibration metric ran from 33 cases on 18 May 2026 to 105 on 24 May 2026 and 598 on 8 June 2026.[^chamla-2026-methods-12] WHO External Situation Report 01 (as of 18 May 2026) documented 528 cumulative suspected cases, 132 deaths, and 35 laboratory-confirmed cases across DRC and Uganda.[^chamla-2026-introduction-2] Uganda's confirmed case count of 19 as of 4 June 2026 (including five healthcare-worker infections and two deaths) is used to validate the spillover model output.[^chamla-2026-results-7]

**Auxiliary data:** Transmissibility scenario ranges were chosen to span published BDBV estimates from the 2007 Uganda and 2012 DRC outbreaks. The latent period was fixed at 9.7 days and the infectious period at 5.0 days, in line with the broader filovirus literature. The CFR was drawn from a uniform distribution (0.15 to 0.25), centred on the observed confirmed-case ratio of approximately 19% (115 of 598 as of 8 June 2026) and consistent with the historical BDBV range.[^chamla-2026-methods-13] Point-of-entry detection sensitivity values for the spillover model were drawn from WHO Joint External Evaluation assessments; travel-while-infectious parameters (Uganda 12%; South Sudan 15%; Rwanda and Burundi 8%) were applied country-specifically.[^chamla-2026-methods-3] Published reproduction number estimates from previous BDBV outbreaks in Uganda (2007) and DRC (2012), ranging from 1.2 to 2.6, informed scenario boundaries.[^chamla-2026-introduction-3]

### Overview

This analysis produces early estimates of the epidemic size in the Democratic Republic of the Congo as of May 27, 2026, using two independent, scenario-based methods applied under plausible assumptions about surveillance, severity, and epidemic growth.[^mccabe-2026-1] The first method back-calculates cases from reported deaths using historical case fatality ratios and onset-to-death delays; the second estimates outbreak size from confirmed imported cases in Uganda combined with cross-border travel volumes. The two methods yield broadly consistent results, suggesting that approximately 280–2,520 cases of Bundibugyo virus disease may have occurred in DRC as of May 27, 2026.[^mccabe-2026-2]

### Estimate

**Back-calculation from deaths:** Assuming 30% of suspected and confirmed deaths are due to Ebola, a CFR of 33%, and a doubling time of 10 days, the mean estimated outbreak size is 451 cases (95% CI 396–511) as of May 27, 2026.[^mccabe-2026-12] The estimated outbreak size increases with assumptions of a higher proportion of deaths due to Ebola, a lower CFR, and faster epidemic growth.[^mccabe-2026-13] The full scenario range for this method is 306–2,521 cases.[^mccabe-2026-2]

**Travel-based back-calculation:** Assuming Ituri Province as the source population and a doubling time of 10 days, the mean estimated outbreak size is 945 cases (95% CI 196–2,274).[^mccabe-2026-14] The full scenario range for this method is 282–1,345 cases.[^mccabe-2026-2]

**Cross-method synthesis:** Together, the two methods suggest that approximately 280–2,520 cases of Bundibugyo virus disease might have occurred in DRC as of May 27, 2026, with considerable uncertainty around these estimates.[^mccabe-2026-2]

### Methods

**Back-calculation from deaths:** This method links reported suspected and confirmed deaths — assuming a proportion are attributable to Ebola — with estimates of the case fatality ratio (33%, 95% CI 26–40%) and the distribution of time from symptom onset to death (gamma distribution, mean 11.37 days, SD 5.41) derived from previous Bundibugyo virus outbreaks.[^mccabe-2026-6] The method further assumes exponential epidemic growth after a single zoonotic spillover event.[^mccabe-2026-7] It applies to DRC, with transmission assumed concentrated in Ituri and Nord Kivu. Sensitivity analyses vary the epidemic growth rate (main scenario: 10-day doubling time; sensitivity 1: 7-day doubling time; sensitivity 2: 14-day doubling time) and the proportion of suspected and confirmed deaths attributable to Ebola (main scenario: 30%; an additional analysis assumes 100%).[^mccabe-2026-8]

**Travel-based back-calculation (geographical spread model):** This method extends the approach described by Imai and colleagues (2020) during the COVID-19 pandemic, combining information on three confirmed imported BVD cases reported in Uganda as of May 27, 2026, estimates of daily international travel volumes from Ituri and Nord Kivu provinces to Uganda, assumptions about epidemic doubling time, and the presumption that all cases in symptomatic travellers will be detected at the time of recovery or death.[^mccabe-2026-9] The main analysis uses Ituri Province as the source population (n=4,392,200); a sensitivity run expands this to include Nord Kivu (combined n=12,593,600).[^mccabe-2026-10] An additional sensitivity analysis assumes only one imported case rather than three.[^mccabe-2026-11]

### Assumptions

**Proportion of deaths attributable to Ebola:** The percentage of deaths attributable to Ebola is assumed to lie between 30% and 100%.[^mccabe-2026-17] The main scenario uses 30%, with 100% explored in a supplementary analysis. This is a key driver: the estimated outbreak size increases with assumptions of a higher proportion of deaths due to Ebola.[^mccabe-2026-13]

**CFR and onset-to-death distribution:** The CFR is fixed at 33% (95% CI 26–40%) and the onset-to-death distribution is a gamma with mean 11.37 days and SD 5.41, both derived from previous Bundibugyo virus outbreaks.[^mccabe-2026-6] The assumption that these parameters are consistent with those from prior outbreaks carries substantial uncertainty.[^mccabe-2026-17]

**Epidemic growth rate:** The current epidemic growth rate is assumed to correspond to a doubling time of between 7 and 14 days, loosely based on early genomic analyses.[^mccabe-2026-17] The main scenario uses a 10-day doubling time; sensitivity analyses use 7- and 14-day doubling times.

**Single zoonotic spillover and exponential growth:** The model assumes exponential epidemic growth after a single zoonotic spillover event.[^mccabe-2026-7] No spatial structure is modelled.

**Geographic concentration of transmission:** Transmission is assumed to be largely concentrated in Ituri and Nord Kivu provinces.[^mccabe-2026-17]

**Travel volumes and traveller detection:** The geographical spread model presumes that all cases in symptomatic travellers will be detected at the time of recovery or death.[^mccabe-2026-15] The scale and patterns of population movement from these provinces to Uganda are assumed to be accurately captured in point-of-entry data.[^mccabe-2026-17]

**Medical-care-seeking bias in imported cases:** At least two of the three imported cases reportedly traveled to Uganda specifically to seek medical care, which the authors flag as a reason to interpret the sensitivity analysis expanding the source population to Nord Kivu with caution.[^mccabe-2026-18]

### Limitations

**Early-stage analysis under scarce data:** The analysis is an early assessment, primarily conducted to inform operational planning, and the estimates may change as more data and information become available.[^mccabe-2026-19]

**Uncertain assumptions with substantial individual uncertainty:** Each of the key assumptions — including the geographic concentration of transmission, the accuracy of travel flow data, the proportion of deaths attributable to Ebola, the applicability of historical CFR and onset-to-death parameters, and the epidemic doubling time — carries substantial uncertainty that may influence the estimates.[^mccabe-2026-20]

**Wide confidence intervals from small numerators:** The travel-based method rests on only three observed imported cases, producing a mean estimate of 945 cases with a 95% CI of 196–2,274[^mccabe-2026-14] — a very wide interval reflecting the small numerator.

**Incomplete and evolving case data:** Testing expanded rapidly for routine and retrospective assessment of suspected cases, deaths are harder to retrospectively assess with investigations ongoing, and suspected cases and deaths are no longer reported in recent situation reports[^mccabe-2026-21] — all of which limit the reliability of the input death counts.

**Medical-care-seeking bias in exported cases:** The findings from the sensitivity analysis expanding the source population should be interpreted with caution, as at least two of the three imported cases reportedly travelled to Uganda specifically to seek medical care[^mccabe-2026-18], making them non-representative of the broader travelling population.

### Data sources

**Primary outbreak data:** The analysis uses 1,031 suspected or confirmed cases and 240 suspected or confirmed deaths reported from 14 health zones across Ituri, Nord Kivu, and Sud Kivu as of May 27, 2026.[^mccabe-2026-3] It also uses 3 confirmed imported cases in Uganda from Ituri as of May 27, 2026 (with a total of 16 Uganda cases confirmed as of June 4, 2026).[^mccabe-2026-5]

**Auxiliary data:** The CFR (33%, 95% CI 26–40%) and the onset-to-death distribution (gamma, mean 11.37 days, SD 5.41) are derived from previous Bundibugyo virus outbreaks.[^mccabe-2026-6] Daily international travel volume estimates from Ituri and Nord Kivu to Uganda are used as inputs to the geographical spread model.[^mccabe-2026-15] The source population sizes are fixed values: Ituri Province at 4,392,200 and Ituri plus Nord Kivu at 12,593,600.[^mccabe-2026-10] The geographical spread method itself extends the analytical framework of Imai and colleagues (2020), developed during the COVID-19 pandemic.[^mccabe-2026-16]

:::{tab-item} Mooring et al. (24 May 2026)
### Mooring et al. (24 May 2026)<br>Modeled Scenario Projections for the Ebola Disease Outbreak Caused by Bundibugyo Virus, 2026

[Read the analysis](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/wr/mm7522e1.htm)

### Overview

This CDC analysis uses a branching process transmission model to project the future trajectory of the 2026 BVD outbreak over a 90-day horizon, estimating how large the outbreak could become under different assumptions about the current number of deaths and different levels of patient isolation. The model was calibrated to three putative cumulative death counts and projected for four possible intervention scenarios ranging from poor (20%) to extremely high (95%) levels of isolation and treatment of symptomatic persons.[^mooring-2026-abstract-1] The central finding is that without high levels of patient isolation, the outbreak is likely to grow very large: with poor isolation levels of patients with BVD (20%) and no other interventions, the likelihood of an outbreak that exceeds 20,000 cases within 3 months is 65%.[^mooring-2026-abstract-2]

### Estimate

This analysis produces forward projections rather than a current true case count estimate; it does not provide a detection multiplier or a point estimate of true cases at the data cutoff. The projections are expressed as probabilities of exceeding case or death thresholds within 90 days of May 24, 2026, under three death-calibration scenarios and four isolation scenarios.

Under the **50 deaths calibration**: assuming 20% of infected persons were successfully isolated beginning May 24, 2026, projections showed ≥20,000 cumulative cases in 65% of simulations, ≥10,000 cumulative cases in 85% of simulations, and ≥4,000 cumulative deaths in 69% of simulations.[^mooring-2026-results-1] At 70% isolation, 94% of simulations projected <10,000 cases and only 1% projected ≥20,000 cases; similarly, 90% of simulations projected <2,000 deaths and only 3% projected ≥4,000 deaths.[^mooring-2026-results-2]

Under the **100 deaths calibration**: very large outbreaks were likely in the scenario in which only 20% of patients were isolated (76% of simulations projected ≥20,000 cases and 87% projected ≥4,000 deaths).[^mooring-2026-results-3] In the scenario in which 70% of infected persons were isolated, 73% of simulations projected <2,000 cumulative deaths by August 22, 2026, and 10% projected ≥4,000 deaths.[^mooring-2026-results-4]

Under the **200 deaths calibration**: even with 70% of infected persons isolated, 42% of simulations projected ≥10,000 cases by August 22, 2026.[^mooring-2026-results-5]

The median basic reproductive number R₀ = 2.51 (IQI = 2.27−2.82) in the main analysis calibrated to 50 deaths.[^mooring-2026-discussion-1]

### Methods

**Branching process model:** A branching process model with the capacity to model transmission-reducing nonpharmaceutical interventions was calibrated to three putative cumulative death counts and projected for four possible intervention scenarios.[^mooring-2026-abstract-1] Each simulated outbreak was initialized with one infected person representing a zoonotic spillover event; this person infected a randomly generated number of additional persons based on assumptions about the basic reproductive number (R₀), and any infected persons were added to the simulation at times selected according to the distribution of intervals from one infection to the next, in turn able to cause further infections.[^mooring-2026-methods-1] The model was adapted from one applied to previous viral hemorrhagic fever outbreaks, including a Marburg virus disease outbreak in Ethiopia in 2025.[^mooring-2026-methods-2]

**Calibration to cumulative deaths:** The model was calibrated to three different numbers of cumulative deaths (50, 100, and 200) to account for uncertainty in the current number of deaths caused by BVD; a simulated outbreak was considered compatible with the real-world outbreak if it reached the assumed number of cumulative deaths by May 24, 2026, and if the first death occurred on or before April 24, 2026. Outbreaks were simulated until 500 simulations met these criteria.[^mooring-2026-methods-3] Assumptions for the cumulative number of BVD deaths as of May 24, 2026, were based on publicly available situation reports from DRC.[^mooring-2026-methods-4]

**Scenario projections:** Four intervention scenarios were assessed for each calibration, each implementing a different level of isolation (i.e., percentage of symptomatic infected persons detected, isolated, and treated: 20% [poor], 50% [moderate], 70% [high], and 95% [extremely high]).[^mooring-2026-methods-5] Each simulation reported the cumulative number of cases and cumulative number of deaths from the date of spillover until August 22, which would be 90 days after interventions began.[^mooring-2026-methods-6] The analysis applies to the DRC outbreak broadly, without explicit subnational stratification.

### Assumptions

**Zoonotic seed and single-origin start:** Each simulated outbreak was initialized with one infected person, who represented the person first infected from a zoonotic source (a spillover event).[^mooring-2026-methods-8] This structural assumption of a single spillover constrains inferences about outbreak onset timing.

**Basic reproductive number (R₀):** Each simulated infected person infected a randomly generated number of additional persons based on assumptions about R₀, the average number of persons in a susceptible population infected by an infected person.[^mooring-2026-methods-9] The median R₀ = 2.51 (IQI = 2.27−2.82) in the main analysis calibrated to 50 deaths.[^mooring-2026-discussion-1] Basic reproductive number estimates for Ebola disease vary widely across outbreaks, and the true value of R₀ for this outbreak might be higher or lower than the values used in this analysis.[^mooring-2026-discussion-4] The analysis did not provide evidence that R₀ for this outbreak is unusually large.[^mooring-2026-discussion-5]

**Delay distributions:** Intervals from infection to symptom onset, symptom onset to death, and symptom onset to recovery were held constant for all infections within each simulated outbreak but varied among simulated outbreaks.[^mooring-2026-methods-10] The specific distributional forms and parameter values are not detailed in this document beyond that they were drawn from published Ebola estimates.

**Cumulative deaths at calibration date:** The model was calibrated to three different numbers of cumulative deaths (50, 100, and 200) to account for uncertainty in the current number of deaths caused by BVD.[^mooring-2026-methods-11] The true number of BVD deaths that occurred through May 24, 2026, is unknown; some deaths from BVD might not have been confirmed, and it is possible that other deaths might have been incorrectly attributed to BVD.[^mooring-2026-discussion-6]

**Isolation intervention mechanics:** On the intervention start date, the designated percentage of symptomatic persons was selected to begin isolating, with an average delay of 2 days until isolation and treatment; the same percentage of persons who later developed signs or symptoms was selected to begin isolating with an average delay of 2 days from symptom onset. Simulated persons in isolation were prevented from causing any onward transmission, and the model implicitly assumed that isolated persons who died were safely buried.[^mooring-2026-methods-12]

**No behavioral change:** Changes in behavior that reduce risk for infection (e.g., avoiding contact with ill persons) were not included in the model and might help limit outbreak size.[^mooring-2026-discussion-7]

**No immunity accumulation:** The model did not account for transmission reductions attributable to an increase in the proportion of the population with infection-induced immunity. Given the population size of the communities where this outbreak is occurring, this limitation is unlikely to affect the validity of the projections over the time span and numerical ranges of cases presented in this analysis; however, the model could project unrealistically large outbreaks if applied to longer periods.[^mooring-2026-discussion-8]

**No relapses:** The model did not include infection relapses after recovery, which is unlikely to affect this analysis, but relapses could be important drivers of the course of the epidemic over a longer period.[^mooring-2026-discussion-9]

### Limitations

**Uncertainty in current death counts:** The true number of BVD deaths that occurred through May 24, 2026, is unknown. Some deaths from BVD might not have been confirmed; similarly, it is possible that other deaths might have been incorrectly attributed to BVD.[^mooring-2026-discussion-6] The analysis addresses this by calibrating to three different death counts (50, 100, 200) rather than resolving the uncertainty.

**R₀ uncertainty:** Basic reproductive number estimates for Ebola disease vary widely across outbreaks, and the true value of R₀ for this outbreak might be higher or lower than the values used in this analysis. High-quality data on changes in the number of cases and deaths over time are essential to more precisely estimate R₀.[^mooring-2026-discussion-10]

**Behavioral change not modelled:** Changes in behavior that reduce risk for infection (e.g., avoiding contact with ill persons) were not included in the model and might help limit outbreak size.[^mooring-2026-discussion-7]

**No immunity depletion:** The model did not account for transmission reductions attributable to an increase in the proportion of the population with infection-induced immunity, which is unlikely to affect projections over the time span and numerical ranges presented but could cause the model to project unrealistically large outbreaks if applied to longer periods.[^mooring-2026-discussion-8]

**No relapses:** The model did not include infection relapses after recovery, which is unlikely to affect this analysis, but relapses could be important drivers of the course of the epidemic over a longer period.[^mooring-2026-discussion-9]

### Data sources

**Primary outbreak data:** Assumptions for the cumulative number of BVD deaths as of May 24, 2026, were based on publicly available situation reports from DRC.[^mooring-2026-methods-4] The DRC national public health institute reported 10 confirmed deaths and 223 suspected deaths as of May 24, 2026.[^mooring-2026-discussion-2] A subsequent report with data as of June 2, 2026, reported a cumulative total of 62 confirmed deaths in DRC.[^mooring-2026-discussion-3] These reports drove the three calibration targets (50, 100, and 200 deaths), used to bracket uncertainty about true death counts.

**Auxiliary data (parameters from prior outbreaks):** Assumptions about parameters were based on published estimates from previous Ebola outbreaks; estimates specific to BVD were used when available.[^mooring-2026-methods-7] The analysis does not specify by name which published studies or datasets were used for delay distributions or other parameters. No travel data, demographic data, or line list data from the current outbreak are mentioned as inputs.

:::{dropdown} Methods overview

These pages are produced by a semi-automated evidence-synthesis pipeline that pairs AI models with human review at each major checkpoint: one model reads each source analysis on its own and grounds every extracted claim in a verbatim quoted passage; a second model takes the resulting structured comparison as input, and drafts the narrative. Human analysts then review and edit the output to ensure accuracy and completeness prior to publishing.

**Identifying analyses.** Analyses are identified through continuous monitoring of preprint servers, peer-reviewed publications, and grey literature. An analysis is included if it directly addresses the 2026 Bundibugyo virus disease (BVD) outbreak and provides a quantitative estimate relevant to a given topic (e.g., outbreak size, risk of spread). Each included source is logged, and if the authors later release an updated version, the newer version supersedes the earlier one.

**Preparing the source text.** Each source text, regardless of source type (e.g., PDF, HTML), is converted to Markdown. A human analyst then curates the converted file and removes sections that carry no evidentiary content, such as references, acknowledgments, author contributions, affiliations, and declarations of interest, to keep extraction focused and reduce the number of input tokens used.

**Extracting structured evidence.** The curated text is split into individually citeable units, one per sentence, and passed to Claude Sonnet 5-5. For each of a fixed set of dimensions (e.g., estimates, methods, limitations), the model answers from that one source alone and must ground every factual claim in a verbatim span of its text.

**Synthesizing the narrative.** OpenAI's GPT-5.5 model reads the dimensions extracted for each analysis and synthesizes the information, covering the state of the evidence, how the estimates compare and why they diverge, the key uncertainties and assumptions shared across analyses, what would most reduce that uncertainty, and what it implies for the response.

**Human review and publication.** Human analysts edit the draft for accuracy, completeness, and tone prior to publishing.

**Limitations.** This pipeline surfaces only publicly available analyses; works that have not been made public are not captured.
